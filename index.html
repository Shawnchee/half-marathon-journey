Half-marathon plan

Weekly targets · not completed runs

Three easy runs per week · no football. Leave at least one non-running day between runs. Start from the logged 3 km; build from what you actually complete.

Easy 1 & 2: 6:50–7:45/km. Longer easy: 7:00–8:00/km. Provisional guides only: comfortable conversation and effort about 3–4/10 come first. Slower or walk breaks are fine.

Goal: sub-6:00/km. Readiness: not yet established. Race date fixed: 13 December. Assess the pace goal by 25 November using new logs, endurance and recovery. This starting build alone does not establish readiness for 21.1 km; race pace remains undecided.

Progression & race decision

Follow recovery, not deadlines: Start gently for 5–10 minutes. Repeat a week if you are unusually tired or not recovering normally. Stop for pain that worsens or changes your stride. Missed sessions are not a debt. The first window includes the completed 22 October run; do not cram remaining sessions into its last days.

Why the small steps? Only one recent 3 km is documented. These are cautious working targets, not a claim that 3 km is your true maximum. A large observational study linked abrupt increases in a single outing to more injuries; it does not prove a universal safe percentage. We can revise the distances as new logs establish your actual capacity.

How sub-6 becomes better supported: First establish consistent, comfortable running. Then allow a longer preparation block to build endurance, usually including longer outings toward 14–16 km if tolerated. Only after a stable base, introduce one controlled goal-pace session per week in place of an easy session. Recent sustained pace, endurance, and recovery must agree; one fast 5 km or an old PB cannot establish race readiness.

Decision by 25 November: Keep 13 December fixed. Review consistent running, comfortable longer outings, recovery and any controlled pace practice supported by the base. Sub-6:00/km stays the target only if the evidence supports it; otherwise revise the race pace. Do not force a long test or assume run/walk makes insufficient preparation safe. If endurance or health does not support starting, reassess participation separately.

Longer-term preparation: Think in months rather than squeezing more work into seven weeks. Once a comfortable base is established, allow roughly 12 or more weeks of individualised half-marathon preparation, with easier weeks and a taper. This is planning context, not a promised completion date. Keep the three-run routine, and add only light, familiar strength work when recovered.
