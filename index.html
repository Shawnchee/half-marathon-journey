Target: sub-6:00/km · assess by 25 November. The 3 km was a chosen distance, not a fitness limit. This revised build targets a 12–14 km peak long run if recovery supports it. Endurance and race pace still need confirmation from new training; the schedule does not guarantee the goal.

How to progress & assess race pace

Start with a useful check: Next longer outing: 4 km easy; extend toward 5–6 only if still comfortable. Do not turn it into a test. The October 22 run remains recorded as 3 km at 6:46/km, effort 4/10. The first window includes it; do not squeeze missed runs into the remaining days.

Earn the next step: Start each run gently. Choose the lower distance first; extend only after comfortable completion and normal recovery the next day. Repeat or reduce when needed. Leave a non-running day between runs and at least 48 hours between controlled pace and the long run. Stop for worsening pain or altered gait. No catch-up kilometres.

Keep most running easy: Pace ranges are provisional guides, not tested zones. Controlled blocks are optional, comfortably hard at most (about 5–6/10), never an all-out test. Warm up and cool down easily within the listed distance. If effort is too high or the base is inconsistent, run Easy 2 entirely easy. The long run stays easy throughout.

25 November checkpoint: Look for consistent training, a comfortable 10–12 km outing, controlled goal-pace blocks without straining, and normal recovery. These are coaching checks, not validated pass/fail thresholds. Short-run speed alone does not establish 21.1 km endurance. Review again after the peak outing and before race day; revise race pace if the evidence does not support sub-6.

Peak, then taper: Aim for 12–14 km around 28–30 November only after the preceding distances are comfortable. Do not force 16–21 km to prove readiness. Reduce training in the final windows. For longer outings, practise familiar fuel and fluids; for 1–2.5 hours, general carbohydrate guidance is 30–60 g/hour, adjusted for tolerance. Slow down in heat and on hills.

Race approach: 13 December stays fixed. A 5:55–5:59/km average is the goal only if training supports it. Do not start faster to bank time. Slower pacing or run/walk does not automatically resolve inadequate endurance; reassess starting if health or preparation does not support the distance.
