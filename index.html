Half-marathon plan

Weekly targets · not completed runs

Two priority runs: Easy 1 + the longer run. Easy 2 is optional. Move the days around football; progress only when recovered.

Paces below are provisional guides from the 22 Oct baseline, not mandatory limits. Easy effort (about 3–4/10) and comfortable conversation come first: slower or walk breaks are fine. Race pace is a stretch goal, not a confirmed recommendation.

52 days is a short preparation window. These adjustable targets do not guarantee half-marathon readiness or sub-6:00 pace. Reassess the race after the longer outings; run/walk or postponing remain options.

How to follow the plan

Keep it easy: Start gently for 5–10 minutes and finish the longer run easy. Judge effort by comfortable conversation; slow down or walk in heat and on hills. The pace ranges are guides, not lower speed limits.

Manage the whole week: Football is a hard session. Leave recovery space around it and the longer run; drop optional Easy 2 when tired. Keep at least one full rest day. Repeat a week when recovery is poor instead of catching up. Stop if pain worsens or changes your stride.

Practise, don’t experiment: On outings lasting roughly 75–90 minutes or more, practise familiar carbohydrate and fluid intake. For 1–2.5-hour endurance exercise, general guidance is 30–60 g carbohydrate/hour; begin near the lower end if tolerated. Use water as directed with gels, check labels, and avoid forcing excessive fluid. Add only light, familiar strength work if recovering well.

Race checkpoint: Reassess the goal by 25 November. Short-run speed is not proof of 21.1 km endurance. Start controlled; do not run faster early to bank time. The listed 5:55–5:59/km is a stretch goal, not a current prescription. A finish-focused run/walk approach or postponing remains an option.
